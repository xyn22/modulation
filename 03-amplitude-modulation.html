Lesson 3: Putting the message onto the carrier (AM)

In lesson 2 the carrier always had the same height. Now we let the message control that height. This is AM, amplitude modulation: "amplitude" because we change the carrier's height, "modulation" because we change it on purpose to carry information.

1. The message

2. The carrier, before modulation (constant height)

3. The AM signal: the carrier's height now follows the message

What is happening

Compare plot 1 and plot 3 at the same moment in time:

  1. When the message is at its highest point, the carrier in plot 3 is at its tallest.
  2. When the message is at its lowest point, the carrier is at its shortest.
  3. In between, the carrier's height smoothly follows the message up and down.

The carrier still wiggles at its own fast frequency. Only its height changes. Draw a line along the tops of the peaks and you get the envelope, which has exactly the same shape as the message. The message is now "riding" on the carrier.

Modulation depth

Depth says how strongly the message pushes the carrier's height around. The carrier's normal height is 1:

  • 0%: the message has no effect. You are back to the plain carrier from lesson 2, which carries no information.
  • 50%: the height swings between 0.5 and 1.5, half its normal size in each direction.
  • 100%: the height swings between 0 and 2. At the message's lowest point the carrier shrinks to nothing for a moment.

More depth makes the message easier for a receiver to pick out. Lesson 4 shows what happens if you push it past 100%.

Building the formula, one step at a time

Lesson 1 said a wave is "height × sin(2π · f · t)". AM just makes the height change over time.

carrier = 1 · sin(2π · fc · t)  fixed height 1
height(t) = 1 + depth · sin(2π · fm · t)  normal height, pushed up and down by the message
AM signal = height(t) · sin(2π · fc · t)

That is all AM is: multiply the fast carrier by a height that follows the slow message. Here fc is the carrier frequency, fm is the message frequency, and depth is written as a number from 0 to 1 (so 50% = 0.5). In textbooks depth is usually called the modulation index and written as the Greek letter μ ("mu").

Try this
  1. Set the depth to 0%. Plot 3 looks exactly like plot 2: no message is being sent.
  2. Slowly raise the depth to 100% and watch the envelope grow until its lowest points touch the middle line.
  3. Change the message frequency. The envelope changes with it, but the fast wiggles inside stay the same.
  4. Lower the carrier frequency to 10 Hz. With so few wiggles, the envelope becomes hard to see. This is why the carrier must be much faster than the message.
