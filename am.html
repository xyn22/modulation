1. Message m(t) = Am cos(2π fm t)

The information you want to send (e.g. audio). It is slow compared to the carrier.

2. Carrier c(t) = Ac cos(2π fc t)

A fast, pure sinusoid. On its own it carries no information; it is the "vehicle".

3. AM signal s(t) and its envelope

The carrier's amplitude follows Ac(1 + μ cos 2π fmt). When μ > 1 that term goes negative, the carrier phase flips, and the envelope no longer matches the message.

s(t) ±Ac(1 + μ cos 2π fmt), ideal envelope

4. What an envelope detector recovers

A simple receiver (diode + RC filter) outputs |Ac(1 + μ m̂(t))|. It matches the ideal envelope only while μ ≤ 1.

Detected envelope |Ac(1 + μ cos 2π fmt)| Ideal Ac(1 + μ cos 2π fmt)

5. Amplitude spectrum |S(f)| (single-sided)

Expanding the formula gives three pure tones: the carrier at fc with amplitude Ac, and two sidebands at fc ± fm with amplitude Acμ/2 each. The information lives only in the sidebands.
